## Judgment

E0149 is a useful lane-based structural and provenance audit, but it is not a
Fortran 2023 conformance comparison. The LFortran file is an independent
parser-architecture reference, not a normative language oracle. The
post-repair replay must be distinguished from the immutable pre-repair
baseline.

## Required corrections

* R000001 is the pre-repair baseline. M005 and M006 are repaired in
  R000018 by `standard-new` `424853273a9c424d0483303478a794090756aa80`;
  they must not remain described as current defects.
* The 21-row matrix is a manual anchor and divergence ledger, not a complete
  one-to-one adjudication of 1,346 heads and 2,835 alternatives. The generated
  production-coverage.tsv now inventories every production head and its
  alternative count; language equivalence remains a separate corpus gate.
* Bison acceptance and conflict counts validate projection generation and
  parser-engineering shape only. They do not establish standard conformance,
  lexer behavior, semantic constraints, diagnostics, or runtime behavior.
* The all-root `standardir_start` export is a closure grammar, not the
  production parser entry point. Its conflict counts must not be presented as
  a direct quality ranking against LFortran's selected `units` parser.

## Genuine StandardIR advantages

StandardIR retains J3-24-007 rule, page, byte and source-hash lineage on
generated alternatives; the pinned LFortran Bison source has no equivalent
normative provenance. StandardIR also preserves normative role distinctions
and does not copy LFortran actions or extensions into the source IR. These are
real advantages for the project's specification/provenance claim, without
implying better parser runtime behavior.

## Scope

The comparison is adequate as a structural audit once the baseline/replay
distinction and inventory limitation are explicit. Remaining work is generic
target factoring, root selection, lexer-contract declaration, duplicate
lineage handling, and normalization witnesses.
