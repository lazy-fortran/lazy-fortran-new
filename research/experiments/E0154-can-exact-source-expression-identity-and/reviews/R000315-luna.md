not a complete or directly usable Fortran 2023 parser. It identified:

* a possible Unicode-minus leakage at EBNF lines 1323--1325 and 2397--2398,

implementation extensions. It confirmed the StandardIR advantages of
normative source/page/byte/hash lineage, complete source-alternative coverage,

## Coordinator adjudication

The independent deterministic comparison in E0155 refines the Unicode finding.
The source glyphs must remain in comments and provenance, but target bodies must
use the canonical spellings from the lexical facts. In R000314:

* EBNF still emits U+2013 in the bodies of `R1010`, `R712` and `R868` at
  `grammar.ebnf:1325`, `2861` and `2398`, and U+2019 in character-literal
  bodies at `grammar.ebnf:1450`, `1541`, `2143` and `2532`. This is a real
  target projection defect under D0090/D0091.
* The ANTLR4, Bison and tree-sitter lexical projections visibly map the same
  source facts to ASCII `-` and `'` (`Fortran2023.g4:4953--4955`,
  `fortran2023.y:8`, and `grammar.js:3113--3116`). Their comments retain the
  original Unicode scalars. The broad review concern is therefore not evidence
  that all four target bodies leak Unicode; the EBNF body is the confirmed
  failure and the cross-format lexical witness is the required next gate.

This review is evidence for deterministic follow-up only. It does not alter
