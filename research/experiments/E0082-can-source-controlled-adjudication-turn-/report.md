## Question

Can source-controlled adjudication turn semantic candidates into typed
evidence without promoting constraint mentions to aliases?

## Method

The analyzer reruns E0081 against the pinned canonical text, then joins every
candidate span to a small source-controlled policy. Exact definitional and
relational witnesses become typed resolution records. Other candidate spans
are retained as constraint evidence with an unresolved body. The procedure
does not emit parser projections.

It also records every numbered constraint associated with the pinned Core 0
closure. Those records retain their source line, page, hash and
`unresolved-body` status. A separate key traversal compares the complete
candidate and constraint inventories with the outputs. The source-controlled
policy witnesses are searched in the canonical text, and a controlled phrase
mutation is required to fail.

The accepted records are limited to the exact policy witnesses. The retained
modal records are evidence for later predicate formalization, not semantic
facts. In particular, the experiment does not infer that a term is an alias
merely because it occurs in a constraint sentence.

## Boundary

E0082 establishes a deterministic source-controlled adjudication boundary:
explicit definition and relation witnesses can become typed,
source-provenanced StandardIR resolution facts, while modal occurrences remain
unresolved until their predicate bodies are formalized. The result does not
measure semantic-rule acceptance or model capability. The next slice is to
formalize a bounded set of retained constraint bodies mechanically, preserving
unresolved and disputed states and testing fact dependencies before any model
escalation.
